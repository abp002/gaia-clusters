README GitHub

Open clusters in Gaia DR3

Stars born together share position, distance and motion. HDBSCAN finds them as dense clumps in Gaia DR3; the catalogue of Hunt & Reffert (2023) says which ones it should have found.

How to read it

Where things sit

Each grey star is at its distance from Bailer-Jones et al. (2021), not at one over its parallax, which is biased when the parallax is uncertain. The Sun is at the tip of the wedge, and the arcs mark every kiloparsec.

The members of a recovered cluster sit at the cluster's distance in Hunt & Reffert. In these regions, a member's own distance is uncertain by about 100 pc (median), while half of a cluster's members sit within about 3 pc of its centre (median). At their own distances, clusters would turn into needles pointing at the Sun. So a cluster's depth is not shown: seen side-on, it is flat.

What is drawn

A cluster is drawn at its catalogue position, as wide as its tidal radius in Hunt & Reffert, and never smaller than a few pixels: a sphere if HDBSCAN recovered it, a ring if not. Point at a sphere and it turns into its stars: the group of the HDBSCAN run that recovers it best, with the highest recovery × purity. A missed cluster's card shows the run that came closest.

The colour-magnitude diagram in each card shows the group's stars that Hunt & Reffert also list (blue), the group's other stars (red) and the members it misses (grey). Blue against red is purity; blue against grey, recovery.

The groups that no Hunt & Reffert cluster owns are hidden unless you ask for them. Without a significance test they are not clusters.

Level of detail

The field is stored in random order and sent in nested levels: 65,536 stars, then twice as many each time. Every level is a uniform sample of the field, so the page shows the first one at once, adds the rest as they arrive, and draws fewer stars while the view moves.

This is not a discovery: Hunt & Reffert ran the same method on the same data. The score and how it was fixed in advance are in the README.
